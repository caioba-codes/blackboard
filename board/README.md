# The board page: how it behaves

Reference for changing `board.html.tmpl` and `bridge.py`. The agent writing lessons does not need it: SKILL.md covers authoring. Describe behavior here when it changes, and keep SKILL.md to what a lesson writer must do.

## Layout and navigation

- **One bridge, every workspace.** `bridge.py` on 7331 serves every course. Each workspace has a slug (the directory above `learn`) and its own current lesson; `render.sh` pins the board tab to `/w/<slug>/`, so two sessions rendering different courses never clobber each other. `/lessons` is the hub across every folder (search, folder chips, Recent, this folder, other folders on this computer). `/w/<slug>/lessons/NNNN#e3` links to a lesson and a note, `#s-<heading-slug>` to a section.
- **Top bar.** Left: an **All lessons** link and the zoom control (− 100% +, keys `-` `+` `0`, 70% to 200% of the lesson column, remembered in this browser). Center: full-text search over every lesson in every folder (`/api/search`), showing the matching line with the words highlighted and its section, opening the lesson scrolled there (key `/`, arrows and Enter). The hub's search box uses the same search. Right: the XP health bar and the chat button.
- **XP health bar.** One segment per quiz item, in the lesson's accent (gold when full). XP counts multiple choice right, check-ins marked **Got it** and flashcards graded 4 or 5. The bar is the Contents button: click it or press `t` to open the **level map**, a card that drops from under the button on the left (so it sits beside the docked chat): each chapter is a square node on one dashed path (cleared = filled accent, current = chalk with a pulsing glow and YOU ARE HERE, not started = dim outline; nothing is locked), its score as stars (★★, ★☆), one filled star per multiple choice right or check-in marked Got it, the current chapter's `###` sections as small sub-nodes, and the path filled up to the reading position. A Practice Exam chapter is the BOSS tile, a Cheat Sheet chapter the SAVE POINT, the Quiz the final node. Every node is a link, with its state in its accessible name.
- **Chapters only.** The right-edge reading scale (its ticks and hover labels) and the level map list only the lesson's `## Chapter N · …` headings (the cheat-sheet chapter included) and `## Quiz`; the topics breakdown, Sources and any other `##` stay out. A lesson with no `Chapter` headings keeps every `##`.
- **Reading scale.** Down the right edge, a ruler in the lesson's accent: one mark per `##` at its heading's true place, beside a thin bar filled to the reading position. It measures reading position only, never mastery or XP. Hover shows the title, click jumps. On lesson pages the system scrollbar is hidden and the scale is the scrollbar (drag or click it). A page that fits on screen is full. The hub has no scale.
- **Headings.** `###` subtitles are underlined; bold `**Step N · …**` lead-ins at the start of a paragraph are boxed with a hand-drawn chalk stroke in the accent.
- **Workspaces** are every folder ever rendered plus everything found by scanning the roots in `~/.cache/blackboard/config.json` (`{"scan": ["~/notes", "~/school"]}`; `BLACKBOARD_SCAN=a:b` overrides) for `lessons/` folders.
- **Study sequence.** By default, lesson navigation follows every lesson in a workspace in four-digit ID order. A workspace can put `sequence.json` beside `MISSION.md` to define its current route: `{"version":1,"title":"Exam 3 study sequence","lessons":["0006","0007"]}`. `/api/state` keeps all lessons in `lessons` and adds `sequence: {title, total, lessons}` in the configured order; `/api/state?all=1` also includes each workspace's sequence in `workspaces[]`. The route does not remove older lessons from All lessons. Duplicate or missing IDs are skipped; an absent or unusable route falls back to all lessons. `prev:` in a lesson's front matter names a prerequisite for retrieval practice and does not set navigation order. The board remembers the last opened lesson and video position in browser storage for a Continue link; it never changes quiz records or advances a lesson automatically.
- **Sequence controls.** A lesson shows All lessons, its route position and Next at the top, with titled Previous/Next links at the bottom. On the final lesson, the bottom pager leads back to the overview. The overview offers Continue from the last opened lesson and saved video position; a video ending offers Practice, Next lesson (or Overview), and Replay. These controls remain available in Read, Watch and Both, on mobile and by keyboard. Reading, watching and quiz scores are separate facts: the route never blocks a lesson or marks one complete from a position alone.

## Flags

Like flagging a question on an exam. Every chapter and section heading, every worked example (`**Worked example · …**`) and every question (check-in rows: Your turn, drills, practice-exam parts; multiple choice) gets a small flag in the left margin: shown on hover or keyboard focus, always shown and filled in the accent once flagged. It is a button (`aria-pressed`, "Flag <label>"/"Unflag <label>"); clicking it never opens the answer. Key `f` flags the item with keyboard focus, else the first one on screen; it does nothing while typing, in Watch or full-screen video (where `f` is the player's), in the chat or the source viewer.

The Flagged list shows in three places: a drop-down after Contents and Sources at the top of the lesson, a section at the bottom of the level map (`t`), and **All flagged** on the hub across every lesson and folder (filtered by the hub's search box and folder chips). Each entry has the label (the problem's `Official · …` label, else the heading, the worked example's title or the question's first words), its lesson and chapter, when it was flagged, an optional one-line note (**Note** / **Edit note**, Enter saves, Esc cancels) and ✕ to unflag. Clicking an entry scrolls there and flashes it; hub links are `/w/<slug>/lessons/NNNN#flag=<anchor>`.

Storage: `$WS/learning-records/flags.json` (`{flags: [{id, lesson, anchor, kind, label, chapter, needle, note, added}]}`), read with `GET /api/flags?lesson=NNNN` (`?all=1` for the hub, every workspace) and changed with `POST /flags {ws, op: add|remove|note|move}`. Both refuse other web pages (`foreign()`), unknown workspaces and lessons, and anchors outside `[A-Za-z0-9_.:-]`. Anchors: a heading's `s-<slug>`, a multiple-choice item's `q-<item id>`, else `f-<slug of the label or the question's first words>`. After a rewrite the page finds a flag by anchor, then by the same label, then by the same opening words (`needle`, letters and digits only), and saves the new anchor (`op: move`). One that matches nothing reads **moved or removed**; jumping to it shows its chapter. The hub checks the lesson's markdown instead (heading anchors, `::: mcq` ids, the needle). The Kindle EPUB leaves flags out.

## Being written, and sounds

- **Being written.** Blocks simply appear as they land; nothing is animated. While a lesson is live (`render.sh --live`), the finished chapters show normally and, under them, one progress line ("3 of 7 chapters", and once two chapters have finished, a rough time left from the average time per finished chapter), a thin bar whose width is the only motion, and a quiet row with the title and "being written" for each planned chapter not started yet. The plan is the lesson's `::: contents` block; a chapter counts as finished when the next `##` heading, or the end of the lesson, is written. The bridge computes this (`writing` in `/api/state` and in each SSE `change` event, from `write_progress` in `bridge.py`), timing each heading from when it first saw it.
- **Reduce Motion.** The quiz celebration plays even with Reduce Motion on, because the user asked for it: it is short and never loops. Other transitions (drawer, chat, focus rings) honor Reduce Motion.
- **Quiz celebration.** A right pick plays gold 8-bit pixel sparks, a floating `+1 XP`, a combo counter for consecutive right picks and a square-wave chime; every item right plays a `QUIZ CLEARED` fanfare.
- **Cues** in the same 8-bit voice: a power-on arpeggio when a lesson starts streaming, a blip per new `##` while it streams, a stage-clear jingle when it finishes, an item-get chime when an Explain note arrives, a soft low thud on a wrong pick, a chime or thud when a flashcard is graded. Key `m` mutes all of it, remembered per browser.

## Quiz behavior

- Multiple-choice answers stay changeable: another pick replaces the verdict, and the key is revealed only once picked. A wrong pick shows "Not quite", that option's mistake line and the why's first sentence as a hint; a why without worked steps stays hidden until the right pick.
- Once answered, a quiet **↺ Reset** under the item (keyboard-accessible) clears the pick, verdict and revealed key and takes back that item's XP segment and the combo.
- Every click logs to `learning-records/quiz-log.jsonl` (`mode: mcq`, score 5 or 0); a Reset appends `{mode: "mcq", item, event: "reset"}`. The latest event per item is restored on reload, and a restored pick counts for XP.
- Answer cards: large clickable cards with chalk borders and muted red triangle, blue diamond, gold circle and green square icons by displayed position; a 2×2 grid (one column for long expressions and on narrow screens). Native keyboard-accessible radios, visible focus and non-color correctness indicators.
- After a graded flashcard: `Score N / 5`, one line of rationale, the missing points in dim text. No "great job", no "almost there".

### Self-marking and redo rounds

- Every check-in answer (Your turn, drill, practice-exam part) ends with two quiet buttons, **Got it** and **Missed it**, styled like Reset. One tap records it; tapping the other changes it; the picked one shows pressed (`aria-pressed`). No sound, sparks or streak.
- A tap posts `{kind: "self", item, chapter, result}` to `/ask`, and the bridge appends `{ts, lesson, topic: item, mode: "self", item, chapter, result: "got"|"missed", score: 5|0}` to `learning-records/quiz-log.jsonl`. `chapter` is the `## Chapter …` heading above the item. The score keeps `scores()` and the grid's dots counting it. Multiple-choice lines carry `chapter` too.
- `item` is `c-` and a hash of the row's question and answer text with whitespace collapsed. The same text keeps the same id across reloads and re-renders, an edit makes a new item, and an exact duplicate in the same lesson gets `-2`, `-3` in reading order. Lessons written before self-marking get ids with no change.
- `GET /api/answers?id=NNNN` returns the lesson's item outcome map, `{item: {kind: "self"|"mcq", result: "got"|"missed", chapter, choice?, sig?}}`, from `outcomes()` in `bridge.py`: the latest line per item wins, a multiple-choice Reset clears the item, and a wrong latest pick is missed. The page restores marks from it, keeps it live as marks happen, and counts XP, chapter stars and misses from it.
- While the lesson has misses, one quiet row sits under the title, before Contents: "N missed problems · Redo them", its count live as marks change. No row when nothing is missed.
- **Redo them** shows only the missed items, in lesson order, in place, with every other block hidden. A check-in or a standalone multiple choice shows under the problem it answers (the blocks from its label down, unless a heading, a worked example, a solution step or another question comes between); a multiple choice inside a quiz shows alone. A check-in starts folded, its solution behind the step reveal, then the two buttons; a multiple choice shows its card as it is. The bar on top has the live count, **Next round** and **Back to lesson**. The round is fixed when it opens: a Got it does not pull an item away, Missed it keeps it, and Next round (or reopening) recomputes it. An empty round reads "No misses left." Back to lesson returns to the same scroll position; a level-map or flag jump leaves the round.

### Voice

The Speak button on a flashcard posts to the bridge's `/api/listen`, which runs `bin/listen.sh`: one utterance, Apple Speech, on-device, en-US. The transcript lands in the card's answer box, where it can be edited before Validate. Two presses at once are refused by a lock rather than starting a second recorder. If `listen.sh` exits non-zero the card shows the reason next to the box and typing still works; voice is never the only way to answer.

### The flashcard deck

Modelled on Quizlet's Flashcards mode and Anki's review screen: one card on the stage, a counter, arrows. Never a column of cards.

1. The stage shows one card. Front: the question, and one answer box at the bottom. The student types, or presses **Speak**; Speak posts to `/api/listen`, which runs `bin/listen.sh` on the bridge (on-device Apple Speech) and drops the transcript into the same box, still editable. Enter validates; Shift+Enter is a newline.
2. **Validate** posts `{kind:"grade", item, answer}`. The card breathes while `bin/quiz.py` grades against the `must:` lines with a fast model.
3. The card **turns**. The back shows the `answer:` line (the model answer), then the verdict: the student's words, the score as five pips, one line of rationale, and what was missing. The verdict is painted from the grader's reply at once and also written into the lesson as a `::: score` block, so it survives a reload and feeds the grid's dots.
4. **→** slides the card out to the left and the next one in. **←** goes back; a graded card arrives showing its back, and a click turns it over. **Show answer** turns a card without grading and marks it not graded.
5. After the last card, a summary card: cards, graded, average, how many scored 2 or under. **Start over** returns to the first card; **Generate more** scrolls to the panel.

The deck's position, typed answers and which cards are turned live in page memory keyed by lesson, because every write to the lesson file triggers a full re-render.

Grading is on meaning, not transcription. Speech-to-text noise, missing punctuation and informal phrasing are never errors.

The mic lives on the bridge rather than in the page on purpose: the browser's Web Speech API is Chrome-only and would fail in the cmux WKWebView, while the bridge always runs on the student's own machine.

## Selecting text

- Selecting inside a display equation works like text: a drag across lines selects whole visual lines (Explain receives those rows as TeX); a drag on one line selects just the symbols it covers, going inside a fraction or power when the drag sits inside it (Explain receives e.g. `x^2 (in $x^{2}-x=0$)`). Inline math lights up as a whole formula.
- The selection raises **Explain**, **Definition** (1–5 words only) and **Ask** (opens the chat with the selection quoted).

## Explain callouts

A full-width block below the selected paragraph, framed like chalk: a hand-drawn border (uneven radii) in the accent over a dark translucent fill, a lightbulb and a title in accent chalk, ivory body text, a visible toggle. New callouts open automatically; folding hides only the body, and the whole header stays clickable and keyboard-operable. Clicking or selecting the body does not collapse it. A discreet × removes the block. Loading and error messages show inside the same callout. Writing, folding or removing a callout rewrites the lesson file and rebuilds the page; the rebuild keeps the first visible block at the same screen position, and `#s-…`/`#e…` deep links apply only on the first load. Old `deep` notes render the same way, and old `==…==` markers render as plain text.

## Definition

A one-sentence meaning of the selected term as this lesson uses it, in a small popup at the selection. The text appears at once when it arrives, with no writing animation. It closes on an outside click, Escape or scroll; the selected term keeps a dotted underline that reopens it (Enter/Space too). Anchors persist per workspace and lesson in this browser. Nothing is written to the lesson file and nothing reaches the chat. `POST /define` answers through `bin/define.py`, cheapest source first:

1. **The lesson's own words**: a table row, a `**Term** — …` note, or a `Term:` line, returned verbatim (`via: lesson`).
2. **Precompute**: serving a lesson starts a background pass over its own terms (emphasis used more than once, table keys, inline code, words already asked about; 40 max), one at a time and yielding to real clicks, only when a local model is installed.
3. **Disk cache**: `~/.cache/blackboard/defs/<slug>/<lesson>.json`. Delete it to regenerate.
4. **Local model**: Ollama. `BLACKBOARD_DEFINE_LOCAL` picks the model; by default a plain instruct model (`llama3.2:3b`, `gemma3`), else the smallest installed. Avoid hybrid reasoning models: on Ollama 0.33 neither `think: false` nor `/no_think` silences qwen3. The bridge keeps it warm (`keep_alive` 30 m, a one-token ping at startup and on each page view).

`claude -p` (`BLACKBOARD_DEFINE_MODEL`, default Haiku) is the fallback when Ollama is not running: correct, but 12–20 s. Measured on a K-map lesson: 0.00 s for a term the lesson defines or precompute covered, 0.7 s otherwise, 0.01 s for a repeat after a bridge restart. The definer gets the selected block, the lines around it, other lines using the term, and the lesson title and course. Output is normalized (a leading "A <term> is …" trimmed, `$…$`-wrapped prose unwrapped, a scratchpad opener triggers one stricter retry). Answers are cached per lesson and term, hovering prefetches, and concurrent requests for a term share one result.

## Chat panel

Docks on the right of every lesson page. A round button in the top bar and ⌘⇧. (Ctrl+Shift+. elsewhere) open and close it; the open state carries across lessons and the lesson column moves left. Each lesson has its own Claude Code session, run by `bridge.py` as `claude -p` in the workspace and resumed every turn, with history in `$WS/reference/chat/NNNN.json`; **+** archives that file as `NNNN-<time>.json` and starts fresh. Every turn names the lesson being read and lists its sources (its `::: sources` block, or an older `## Sources`, and `RESOURCES.md`, resolved to files and URLs). Tool steps show live and fold away when the reply ends; the square button stops a reply; a reply keeps running and is saved if the page goes away. Model: `BLACKBOARD_CHAT_MODEL` (default `opus`) at medium effort. It may read only the workspace, TEMPLATES.md and the files the lesson cites, fetch only the sites the lesson cites, and search the web; edits only inside the workspace; anything else is denied without a prompt. Instructions: `chat-system.md`. **Where you are.** Each message also sends `where`: the video's scene when it was played or sought this session (`LessonVideo.context()`: time, chapter, segment, lesson line, section, caption, paused, practice item and whether its solution is revealed), else the `##`/`###` section in view (none in Watch mode, none while a source file is open). `bridge.py` checks and caps every field (`where_line`) and adds `[Looking at: video 2:06:20 · Chapter 5 · … · lesson line 1406 (section “…”) · caption: “…” · paused at the practice “…”, solution not revealed. …]`; `chat-system.md` reads a vague question as about that spot, answers from it without searching the lesson, and hints before answering an unrevealed practice item. A muted chip under the box ("Video 2:06:20 · Ch 5 · Your turn: which gate? (sp12)", or "Reading · Ch 2 · …") shows what the chat will assume; tapping it leaves it out of the next message. It does not load the user's CLAUDE.md, hooks or MCP servers, and only the board's own pages may call it.

**Images.** Paste an image into the box, drop it on the panel, or pick it with the picture button (PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP, at most 6 per message). The page scales anything over 2000 px or 3.5 MB down to a 2000 px JPEG. `POST /chat` takes `images: [{type, data}]` (base64); the bridge saves each one to `$WS/reference/chat/img/NNNN-<id>.<ext>`, lists the names on the user message (`images`), and sends the turn as one `--input-format stream-json` user message with the images as image blocks before the text, so the session sees them and still remembers them on later turns. `GET /api/chat-image?lesson=NNNN&name=F` serves them back for the thread.

## Source viewer

Every file a lesson cites opens over the lesson column, with the chat still docked on the right. The Sources drop-down links its own relative links and adds **Files used**, grouped by folder: the files in the sources block plus each problem's `<!-- source: … -->` comment (with its first page; the `src:` links of problem labels open in the viewer but are not listed); a solution file (`…-s2`) brings its exam (`…-e2`). File paths the chat cites (`exams2/fa18-kj-s2.pdf`, page 1) become the same links. PDFs open in the browser's viewer at the page, images fit the width, text and Markdown render in place; the bar has the file name, **Open in new tab** and **Back to lesson** (Esc), and closing restores the scroll position. While a file is open, each chat turn carries `[Viewing: <path>, page N]`, so "this problem" means that file.

`/api/sources?id=NNNN` lists the files, `/api/source-check?p=…` checks chat citations, and `/source/<path>` serves one. `source_file` looks in `sources/`, the workspace, `lessons/` and the course folder, and serves a file only when its real path is inside the course folder, outside hidden folders, with a pdf, image, txt, md, csv or tex extension (no svg or html); extracted text (`x.pdf.txt`) opens the PDF. Other web pages get 403. Missing files show "not found on this Mac"; nothing is copied.

## End card

Every lesson except `plain: true` boards ends with a card: Ultraman Mebius beside a quote of his battle sounds (one of ~30 in `ENDCARD_QUOTES`, picked by workspace slug + lesson id, so a lesson always shows the same one), and a **Color timer** button that plays an 8-bit "ning nung" loop (click only, `m` mutes it). It stays out of the rail, contents map, search, Explain/Definition and XP, and hides while a lesson is streaming. The Kindle EPUB gets it as its last page.

The photo is local and never part of the skill: `bridge.py` serves `/end-card.jpg` from `BLACKBOARD_END_CARD`, else `"end_card"` in `~/.cache/blackboard/config.json` (`{"end_card": "~/Pictures/mebius.jpg"}`), else `~/.config/blackboard/end-card.jpg`, and refuses a path inside the skill folder. With no photo the card shows the quote alone. Never commit the image: it is someone else's copyright.

## Video keys

The lesson video takes YouTube's keys: Space or `k` play and pause, `j`/`l` jump 10 s, ←/→ 5 s, ↑/↓ volume, `m` mute, `f` full screen, `0`–`9` jump to that tenth of the video, Home/End, `c` captions, `i` picture in picture, `<`/`>` speed, Shift+←/→ and `[`/`]` the previous and next scene, `r` replay the scene. They work when the player has focus, in Watch and in full screen. The view switch has two views, Read and Watch. A lesson whose video is made chapter by chapter (VIDEO.md **Chapter videos**) gets one row under each chapter heading in Read: **Play this chapter's video** when that chapter's video is installed (it opens Watch at the chapter's start), else a quiet "Video being made" row with that chapter's own progress and time left (`/api/video-status?id=NNNN&chapter=N`, polled every 10 s). Watch plays the installed chapter videos back to back as one playlist with one seek bar: the bridge joins their maps (`/api/lesson-video` returns `parts`, each with its offset, and `chapters`), the player's single `<video>` loads the part that holds each time, and the captions come joined from `/api/lesson-video-captions`. The intro line names the chapters whose video is not available yet. Files are never joined for the board. No other per-section play buttons. They never fire while typing, in the chat, on the contents map or search, or on a button, link or quiz control.

## Video progress card

While a lesson's video is being made, the lesson page shows a small card where the video's header card will go: VIDEO IN PROGRESS with an overall percent and bar, the current stage with its real counts ("Narration · 412 / 1,899 lines · 21%", "Render · 37 / 247 chunks · 15%"), "Stage 2 of 6 · about 1 h 4 min left in narration", and the six stages (Script, Narration, Scenes, Render, Review, Import) with the finished ones ticked. It updates every 10 s without a reload (skipped while the tab is hidden) and gives way to the player once the video is imported. The only motion is the bar's width.

`lesson-video.js` asks `/api/lesson-video` first; with no installed video it polls `GET /api/video-status?id=NNNN` (`&ws=<slug>`; 403 to other web pages). The bridge answers from `bin/video_status.py status`, computed from the files of the project that `$WS/reference/video-project-NNNN.json` points to (written by `video_pipeline.py init`, see VIDEO.md):

```json
{"active": true, "installed": false, "lesson": "0008", "project": "/abs/project", "stage": "narration",
 "stageIndex": 2, "stageCount": 6, "percent": 17.5, "eta": 4773, "stalled": false,
 "lastProgress": 1794173095, "now": 1794173098, "weights": {"script": 10, "narration": 30, "scenes": 15, "render": 35, "review": 5, "import": 5},
 "stages": [{"key": "narration", "label": "Narration", "state": "current", "done": 472, "total": 1899, "unit": "lines", "percent": 24.9}]}
```

`stages` lists all six (`state` done, current or todo; `total` null while unknown, such as before `SPLIT.json` exists; render may add `note: "joining and muxing"` once every chunk is done). `percent` is the weighted sum of each stage's fraction; the weights put the machine-bound narration and render first. `eta` is seconds left in the current stage only, from the spacing of the mtimes of its last 200 finished files (null with fewer than 3). No new file for more than max(10 min, 10 × that spacing) sets `stalled`, and the card says "no new progress for N min" instead. `{"active": false, "installed": true}` once the board validates the imported video, `{"active": false}` with no pointer or a deleted project; either removes the card.

## Slate surface

`board: slate` in front matter gives a real-blackboard surface: `slate.jpg` ("Black smooth textured paper background" from magnific.com, Freepik free license: credit it when the skill is shared publicly), stacked on its mirror so it tiles and scrolls with the text, with a faint chalk bloom and grain (`chalk.png`) on text, math and diagrams. A `slate-custom.jpg` next to it replaces the photo. On trial; it may become the default.
