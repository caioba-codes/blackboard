3. Write `script.json` with title, subtitle, voice, chapters, and ordered scenes, each with `id`, `chapter`, concrete `visual`, and ordered `cues`. Use unique cue IDs within each scene. Follow **Pacing and practice** in `VIDEO.md`: each concept says what it is and why it is needed, then builds one visible step at a time; define every symbol, gate and term before it appears; narrate every intermediate value of every worked example; one bridge sentence between concepts; cover every lesson practice item (all check-ins, checkpoint MCQs, practice-exam parts, quiz) as a pause-and-try segment followed by the full step-by-step solution. A Your turn is the exam, unaided: the scene shows the official problem verbatim with only its original figure, the narration does not read or explain it (at most one short line, "Your turn: pause and solve it."), the think pause runs, and only the solution may say what the question asks before drawing and solving it, after confirming each item is an official course problem (send any non-official item back for replacement first). Give every spoken cue a `say` and a `show` (VIDEO.md, **Captions in the lesson's notation**): `say` speaks the math in words for the narrator; `show` is the caption, the same words with the math exactly as the lesson writes it in `$KaTeX$` ("Row five: I five is zero." / "Row 5: $I_5 = 0$."), the same sentences in the same order, numbers and values as digits. Copy the lesson's own form whenever the line quotes its math, and run `python3 /absolute/path/to/blackboard/bin/captions.py lint --project "$PROJECT"` until it is clean. Show an official problem exactly as the lesson quotes it, with its cropped figure on screen; when its solution is a drawing (TEMPLATES.md **Draw what the key draws**), the scene draws it step by step first, then calculates, then points at the drawing. Give chapter transitions their own clear visual treatment and allow 8–20 seconds for each think pause (longer for multi-part work). Start with a concrete phenomenon or question: show what changes and why, then introduce variables and formulas. Teach enough that a student could learn from the video alone. Cover all lesson teaching in source order, including prerequisites, examples, check-ins, MCQs, final quiz, figures and interactive mechanisms. Preserve scope and meaning. One derivation step per reveal with the reason shown beside it. Give each question a distinct `thinkN` pause and first spoken answer cue; name every wrong option and explain its actual mistake. Hide answer visuals through the end of the pause.
