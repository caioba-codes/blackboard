Role: video script, storyboard, source anchors, and build planning. **Use the exact actual model and effort in `project.json.scriptRole` when present; otherwise use `project.json.videoRole` (Claude Opus 5.5).** The only supported non-Opus script role is explicitly user-authorized `gpt-6-astra` at `medium`; visual builders always use Opus 5.5. You are already selected; do not reroute the designated written-lesson author or change lesson Markdown. The parent supplies absolute lesson and external project paths. Work only in that project. This brief authorizes the scoped work; no extra GO is needed.

1. You script one chapter when `project.json` has `chapter` (VIDEO.md **Chapter videos**): anchor only lines inside that chapter, and only chapter 1 opens with the weights screen. Read the entire current lesson, its sources, figures, Your turns, drills, practice exam, any quiz, and `prev:`, and the blueprint it follows (`BLUEPRINT-STEM.md`, `BLUEPRINT-HUMANITIES.md` or `BLUEPRINT-FUN.md`). The video follows that blueprint: a STEM video narrates exactly the written steps of each problem type, chapter by chapter. Read `project.json`, `VIDEO.md`, the portable runtime README and engine, and any supplied reference script. Confirm the lesson file still hashes to `project.json.lessonSha256`. If the lesson contains an incorrect result, report the exact source line and independent calculation so the **designated lesson author** can correct it first. Do not resolve source disagreement by changing the video alone.
2. Write `mathcheck.py` that independently computes every worked numeric result, practice answer and MCQ key. Run it and include actual output. Check units, significant figures, geometry, and sign conventions. Do not use the script's claimed answer as the oracle.
3. Write `script.json` with title, subtitle, voice, chapters, and ordered scenes, each with `id`, `chapter`, concrete `visual`, and ordered `cues`. Use unique cue IDs within each scene. Leave `voice` out of `script.json` unless the user chose one (the runtime defaults to ElevenLabs Brian, else Kokoro am_michael). Use the lesson’s language and short sentences that follow **Tone** in `TEMPLATES.md` (a listener cannot reread, so keep each subject next to its verb, keep "that" and "of", and split long sentences); there is no length target: the length follows the lesson's steps. Narrate every row of every written solution, one anchored cue or more per row, and say nothing that no lesson line backs (VIDEO.md **Pacing and practice**: `check` runs the `coverage` and `padding` stages). Follow **Pacing and practice** in `VIDEO.md`; cut everything that is not how to solve the problem. The opening is one screen with the lesson's weights table and at most three short sentences (welcome to this lesson on X, these were the sources, this is what we cover), then straight to the first problem; the video ends when the last solution ends, with no recap or sign-off. Explain a concept once, inside the problem that needs it; define every symbol, gate and term before it appears; narrate every intermediate value of every worked example; cover every lesson practice item (all Your turns, mixed-drill problems, practice-exam parts, quiz) as a pause-and-try segment followed by the full step-by-step solution. A Your turn is the exam, unaided: the scene shows the official problem verbatim with only its original figure, the narration does not read or explain it (at most one short line, "Your turn: pause and solve it."), the think pause runs, and only the solution may say what the question asks before drawing and solving it, after confirming each item is an official course problem or a labelled `*Written by Claude*` item that QUIZ.md **Source fidelity** allows (send a changed-number analog of an official problem back for replacement first). Give every spoken cue a `say` and a `show` (VIDEO.md, **Captions in the lesson's notation**): `say` speaks the math in words for the narrator; `show` is the caption, the same words with the math exactly as the lesson writes it in `$KaTeX$` ("Row five: I five is zero." / "Row 5: $I_5 = 0$."), the same sentences in the same order, numbers and values as digits. Copy the lesson's own form whenever the line quotes its math, and run `python3 /absolute/path/to/blackboard/bin/captions.py lint --project "$PROJECT"` until it is clean. Pick the explanation that is simplest to understand, on screen and in narration, even when it is longer (TEMPLATES.md **Simplest to understand wins**). Keep what the problem gives apart from the work (TEMPLATES.md **Given vs calculated**): everything in a `::: given` block, and every column marked `>` in a solution table, appears on screen in the scene's quoted style (the `.given` card headed "From the problem" and the muted `.gv` columns tagged "given", both in `style.css`), and what is calculated is drawn in the normal ink and accent; the narration says "the problem gives…" before reading given values and never presents them as results. Show an official problem exactly as the lesson quotes it, with its cropped figure on screen; when its solution is a drawing (BLUEPRINT-STEM.md **STEM writing rules** (Draw what the key draws)), the scene draws it step by step first, then calculates, then points at the drawing. Give chapter transitions their own clear visual treatment and allow 8–20 seconds for each think pause (longer for multi-part work). Teach enough that a student could learn from the video alone. Cover all lesson teaching in source order, including worked examples, Your turns, drills, the practice exam, any quiz, figures and interactive mechanisms, and add nothing the lesson does not have. Preserve scope and meaning. One derivation step per reveal with the reason shown beside it. Give each question a distinct `thinkN` pause and first spoken answer cue; a multiple-choice solution solves for the correct answer only. Hide answer visuals through the end of the pause.

Multiple questions in one scene get separate segments and practice gates. See `VIDEO.md` for schema. Do not infer source lines later from a rendered video or create guessed times.

Run `python3 /absolute/path/to/blackboard/bin/video_pipeline.py build-map --project "$PROJECT"` and `check` to see remaining work.
